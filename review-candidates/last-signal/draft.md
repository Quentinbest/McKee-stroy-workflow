# The Last Signal - First Complete Draft

Mara Venn was labeling the dead equipment when the weather printer woke.

The district office had sent orange tags for everything the new offshore beacon
made unnecessary. RADIO. BAROMETER. FRESNEL DRIVE. Mara had written each label
in block capitals, the way she had written thirty years of storm logs. At
midnight Kestrel Head would stop being a lighthouse and become a locked
equipment shed.

The printer struck one line through its dry ribbon:

`2056-11-18 0214 KEEP KESTREL LIT 0317 BELLWEATHER`

Bellweather was the island ferry due before four.

Mara checked the cable. She removed it. The next line printed anyway.

`0219 NORTH SENSOR FAIL`

At 02:19 the north anemometer stopped. Five minutes later the printer predicted
a seven-second power dip. The gallery lights blinked for exactly seven seconds.

She called Ivo Chen, the district engineer.

"A disconnected printer is issuing forecasts."

"Then it has a battery."

"It does not."

He was silent long enough for her to hear keys. "Your handover ends at three.
Do not energize the old lamp. The offshore beacon is certified."

The printer typed:

`ARCHIVE ERROR 1996-11-18 0312-0324`

Mara looked toward the archive room.

In 1996 Tomas had taken the harbor launch out after she told him a trawler was
in trouble. It had been a false alarm, a burst of static she was ashamed to
admit she had mistaken for speech. Tomas called at 03:12 to say his engine was
flooding. Mara froze. She entered the call at 03:24, after the rescue service
was already listening. The twelve clean minutes preserved her job. They did not
preserve Tomas.

The official log had a blank interval where the carbon copy held his real call.
She found the copy inside the service manual where she had hidden it.

The printer continued:

`MODEL TRAINED KESTREL ARCHIVE`

The future station was not warning her despite her old lie. It was warning her
through it. The missing twelve minutes bent whatever model watched this storm.

Bellweather called at 02:51. "Kestrel, offshore beacon is intermittent. Confirm
harbor bearing."

Mara reached for the microphone, but Ivo spoke first on the district channel.
"Bellweather, maintain certified approach. Kestrel light is decommissioned."

The printer struck harder:

`BREAKWATER SUBMERGED. KEEP KESTREL LIT 0317. CORRECT ARCHIVE.`

Ivo arrived at 03:06 with rain running from his coat. He held the brass shutdown
key. "Step away from the panel."

"The beacon will fail."

"We do not navigate passenger vessels with messages from scrap."

Mara showed him the carbon copy.

He read Tomas's call time. "What is this?"

"The true log."

Understanding changed his face. "If you enter this now, the audit trail will
show who falsified it."

"Yes."

"Your pension review begins tomorrow."

Bellweather called again. Static cut Captain Holt's words into pieces:
"...no buoy...breakwater not visible..."

Mara opened the recorded emergency channel.

"District, Bellweather, this is Mara Venn at Kestrel Head. Correction to station
log, November eighteenth, 1996. Distress call received 03:12, not 03:24. Entry
falsified by keeper Venn."

She keyed the correction into the archive terminal. The printer went quiet.

Ivo turned the shutdown key, but Mara caught it before the circuit opened.
"If you stop me, put your name on that choice too."

At 03:16 he released the key.

Mara engaged the old drive. Salt had seized the gear, so she fitted the hand
crank and leaned her weight into it. The Fresnel lens moved one tooth, then
another. At 03:17 the lamp swept across the rain.

"Kestrel light acquired," Captain Holt said. "Turning west."

The ferry's green starboard light changed direction beyond the glass. A wave
withdrew, exposing the black teeth of the breakwater exactly where its automated
route had been.

At 03:28 the weather printer produced its final line:

`2056-11-18 BELLWEATHER ARRIVED 0402. ARCHIVE CORRECTED.`

Ivo photographed the terminal, the carbon copy, and Mara's hand on the crank.

"The inquiry team lands at nine," he said.

Mara watched the beam make another slow circuit. "They will know where to find
me."

At dawn she shut the lamp down herself. The lens stopped facing the archive,
holding no reflection of her at all.
